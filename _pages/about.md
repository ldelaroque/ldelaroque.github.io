I am a PhD researcher in planetary geophysics, focusing on the seismic exploration of icy worlds. My research aims to understand how seismic observations can reveal the internal structure, physical properties, and geological activity of planetary bodies, with a particular focus on Titan.

My PhD research investigates the detectability of seismic events on Titan and their potential implications for the *Dragonfly* mission. I combine numerical modelling of seismic wave propagation with studies of ice attenuation, tidal and flexural stresses, and some other geophysical models to assess what types of seismic signals could be observed at the surface.

More broadly, I am interested in planetary seismology, the interiors and evolution of icy satellites, and the development of geophysical methods for exploring worlds beyond Earth. I am particularly interested in connecting theoretical models and numerical simulations with future planetary missions and observations.

Alongside my research, I enjoy developing open and reproducible computational tools for planetary geophysics and collaborating across disciplines, from planetary science and seismology to experimental and engineering approaches.
